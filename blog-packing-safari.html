What to pack for
a Kruger safari

Nobody has ever come back from a game drive saying they wish they'd packed more clothes. They do come back saying they wish they'd packed a warmer jacket — because a 05:30 open vehicle in June is a different country to the same vehicle at noon.

Colours matter more than you think

Stick to khaki, olive, brown, beige and grey. It isn't a fashion rule — animals are less alert to muted colours, and on a walking safari your guide will insist on it.

  • Avoid white and bright colours — they catch the eye at a distance.
  • Avoid dark blue and black — tsetse flies are drawn to them, and their bite is memorable for the wrong reasons.
  • Avoid camouflage patterns — in several Southern African countries, civilians wearing camo is an offence.

Dress in layers, always

A winter morning drive in the Lowveld can start at 6°C and end at 27°C by the time you're back for breakfast. You want to peel layers off, not tough it out.

  • Thermal or long-sleeve base layer for the early start
  • Fleece or jersey
  • Windproof jacket — the vehicle is open and moving
  • Beanie and gloves from May to August. Yes, really.
  • Light long-sleeve shirts and trousers for the day — they beat sunburn and mosquitoes
The one thing everybody forgets: a headtorch or small torch. Camps are deliberately dark at night, and you'll want to see the path between the boma and your tent.

The rest of the list

  • Binoculars — 8x42 is the sweet spot. One pair per person if you can; sharing is where family arguments start.
  • Camera with a zoom, plus spare batteries and memory cards. Charging points in camps are limited.
  • Sunscreen, wide-brim hat, sunglasses. The winter sun still burns.
  • Insect repellent with DEET, and antihistamine cream.
  • Malaria prophylaxis if your doctor recommends it — the Kruger is a malaria area, highest risk in the wet summer months. Speak to a pharmacist or doctor at least a week before you travel.
  • Personal medication in its original packaging, with a copy of the prescription.
  • Refillable water bottle — you dehydrate faster than you notice.
  • Cash for tips and curios. Guides and trackers are usually tipped separately.

What to leave at home

Hard-shell suitcases if you're flying into a camp by light aircraft — most operators require soft bags and cap luggage at 15–20 kg including hand luggage. Drones are banned in national parks. Loud perfume and aftershave are worth skipping too.

Before you go

Check your park entry booking, take a printed copy of your itinerary in case there's no signal, and tell your consultant about any dietary requirements at least a week ahead — bush kitchens plan their supplies days in advance.
